Robotic Arm Builder

Powered Up Technic Large Hub · 88016

What you're building

A simple 2-motor robotic arm: one motor rotates the whole arm left/right at the base, the second motor lifts the arm up/down at the shoulder. The hub is the LEGO Powered Up Technic Large Hub, element 88016 — a 6-port, rechargeable, Bluetooth LE hub (the same hub used in SPIKE Prime / Mindstorms Robot Inventor sets).

Parts checklist

This app can't tell your exact motor/sensor models apart from a photo, so it treats both as generic Powered Up motors — any Powered Up motor works in any of the hub's 6 ports.

Build steps

  1. Base. Mount motor #1 so its output shaft is vertical and becomes the arm's rotation axis — the simplest base is the arm platform attached directly to that shaft, with the motor's housing fixed to a stationary bottom plate. (If you have a geared Technic turntable ring, you can use that instead and gear the motor into it — sturdier, but not required to get started.)
  2. Shoulder. Mount motor #2 on top of the rotating base platform, with its output shaft horizontal, so it lifts the arm segment up and down. Keep the arm's weight as close to this pivot as you can — Powered Up motors don't strongly hold position against gravity on their own, so a heavy, far-reaching arm will slowly sag. A counterweight opposite the pivot, or keeping the arm short at first, both help.
  3. Arm + end effector. Build the arm segment out from the shoulder motor. With only 2 motors there's none left for a powered gripper, so end it in a fixed hook, scoop, or a simple spring/friction claw that grabs by the arm's own motion.
  4. Mount the hub. Fix the 88016 hub to the non-rotating base (not the rotating platform) if possible, so its cables don't have to twist. If the hub has to sit on the rotating part, route both cables with generous slack and check the Wiring tab's note on cable travel before tightening anything down.
  5. Power up. Charge the hub fully over USB-C first (LEGO ships these hubs at a partial charge). Press the hub's center button to turn it on — its light ring shows charge/status.
  6. Test it. Open the Test tab, connect over Bluetooth, and use the Nudge buttons to confirm which port is the base and which is the shoulder before you button up the build — it's much easier to re-plug a cable now than after everything's screwed down.

Cable routing

The Large Hub has 6 ports total (A–F) around its edges, but this arm only needs two of them. Whichever two you use, leave enough slack in both cables for the base's full rotation range before you lock the arm's beams in place — a taut cable will yank the hub off its mount or stall the base motor mid-turn.

Powering on

Charge the hub fully via its USB-C port before first use. Press the center button once to power on. If the Test tab can't find it, make sure it's on and awake (press the button again) and within Bluetooth range.

On iPhone, open this page in the Bluefy browser app (install it from the App Store) — Safari blocks Web Bluetooth entirely, so this won't work there. On desktop, use Chrome or Edge.
